# PRD Quality Review — Hub Resource Registry

Context applied: internal tool, one operator, Fast path draft, feeds architecture and epics (so Done-ness and Downstream usability weigh more than UJ density).

## Overall verdict
This is a strong draft: it has a real thesis (every contended device in one fair queue, with visible waits and numbers behind "buy another phone"), concrete evidence-backed vision, honest non-goals, tagged assumptions, and testable consequences for most of the engine FRs (FR-1 to FR-17). The risk is concentrated in two places: the Reservation grace/expiry behaviour at the heart of UJ-1 is under-specified and its cost (an idle device) is not surfaced as a trade-off, and the whole platform (registry, queues, Mac Agent, Hub API, Holocene page, queueing math) is presented as one undifferentiated MVP with the phasing living only in the addendum. The Holocene FRs and several thresholds are not yet testable.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The PRD states its decisions as decisions and names what was given up: pure first-come-first-served with "no priority classes in v1" is repeated in FR-7, §6 and §7.2 with a revisit trigger ("Revisit if the Event Log shows first-come-first-served starving something"); partial fairness for pinned runs is admitted in NFR-2. That is above the usual bar.

Where it goes soft is the Open Questions. Q1 ("Is the Reservation grace period ... policy-pinned ... Which value?") is already answered as an `[ASSUMPTION: grace = 10 minutes, policy-pinned]` in FR-9 and the Assumptions Index, so the same item is both assumed and open. Q5 (phone-friendly layout) restates the out-of-scope bullet in §7.2 ("Mobile-friendly access to the page from a phone webview") and answers itself in its own clause ("given Google sign-in blocks a phone webview"). Q4 is a low-stakes tuning knob. Only Q2 (Mac Agent placement vs NFR-3) and Q3 (whether dedicated-QA phones join the default phone Pool) are genuinely open, and Q3 quietly undermines UJ-2, whose agent gets "the S24" from "any Android phone" without saying whether the S24 is dedicated-QA.

The one real tension, Reservation versus FIFO, is not flagged with a `[NOTE FOR PM]`: a Reservation at the head of the Queue holds the Resource idle for up to the grace period (FR-9), which spends utilisation to buy fairness. No counter-metric watches idle time caused by Reservations (SM-C1 watches saturation only).

### Findings
- **high** Reservation expiry and idle cost are undefined (§4.2 FR-9; UJ-1; §9 Q1) — FR-9 says an unclaimed Reservation at the head blocks "anyone behind it until the Reservation's grace period ends", but never says what happens after: does the run drop to the back, lose the Reservation and fail, or re-queue at its original position? A build that runs longer than the wait (the normal case when the plan job queues at position 2) makes this the common path, not an edge. The idle-device cost of the grace window is also unacknowledged. *Fix:* state the post-grace outcome as a consequence, name the idle-time/fairness trade-off in a `[NOTE FOR PM]`, and add an idle-while-reserved counter-metric; close Q1 or drop the assumption.
- **low** Open Questions padded with answered items (§9 Q1, Q5) — see above. *Fix:* delete Q1 and Q5 (keep as assumption and out-of-scope respectively); promote Q3 to a decision before UJ-2 is relied on.

## Substance over theater — strong
Nothing reads as furniture. The three Claimant kinds each drive distinct FRs (deploy jobs in FR-8/9, agents in FR-10, a person in FR-11/26), so there is no persona theater. The Vision names today's concrete failure ("a deploy can wait out its 20-minute limit and fail after its version was already bumped"). NFRs carry numbers or hard rules (NFR-7 200 ms/1 s, NFR-2 pin ordering, NFR-3 no new clock), not boilerplate. The "Innovation" is borrowed openly in the addendum's comparables table, which is the honest framing. One mild exception: the Capacity Estimate (below).

### Findings
- **medium** Capacity Estimate rigor may exceed what two phones can feed (FR-19, UJ-4, SM-5; addendum §B.9, §C) — the flagship UJ-4 example ("9 minutes now and 1.5 minutes with one more, from 212 holds") assumes a sample the hub may take a long time to reach with c=2 phones, and the addendum itself concedes M/M/c is unreliable for "c ≤ 3 and fewer than about 20 holds" and recommends bootstrap or replay instead. The PRD commits to "expected mean wait ... modelled" without saying that the low-data flag will likely be the default state, or that a measured-only view (waits and utilisation, already in FR-18/27) might answer the buy-a-phone question. *Fix:* decide, in the PRD, whether v1 ships the modelled number or measured wait plus replay, and say what Jarad sees when data is low.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and consistent: ordering, visibility, then evidence for capacity. Features map to it, and the addendum's rollout order (Event Log first, so history accrues) is sensible. SM-C1 to SM-C3 are real counter-metrics, which is rare. Weak points: the MVP is a platform-kind scope delivered whole (§7.1 lists all six feature groups, FR-1 to FR-28) with no cut line, even though the addendum §B.10 already knows the order and that the Event Log must ship first. For a single operator that is a lot of surface (HTTP service, a Mac process, a Holocene page, queueing math), and downstream epic creation has no guidance on what is the smallest slice that kills starvation (FR-7 to FR-9, FR-17) versus what is nice-to-have (FR-19, FR-24 to FR-28).

The metrics are partly weak as validators of the thesis (see Findings).

### Findings
- **medium** MVP not sliced; phasing lives only in the addendum (§7.1; addendum §B.10) — everything is "In Scope (v1)". *Fix:* mark a v1a (Event Log, queue-joining deploy jobs, Reservations, CLI compat, status) and v1b (Hub API consumers, Mac Agent, Holocene page, Capacity Estimate) in §7, using the existing rollout order, and say which FRs the starvation fix cannot ship without.
- **medium** SM-1 target is unreachable by design or unmeasurable (SM-1; FR-8, FR-10, FR-20) — "Deploy runs that fail waiting for a Resource. Target: 0 per 30 days" conflicts with a bounded wait (FR-8: wait "shorter than its job timeout") and 48-hour Leases (FR-10) that can legitimately outlast any wait; FR-20 even defines "a deploy job that timed out while waiting" as a normal signal. There is also no baseline for today's failure rate. *Fix:* separate "failed because a Claimant behind it won" (target 0) from "failed behind a legitimately long Hold", and record the current baseline.
- **medium** SM-5 cannot be verified on any schedule (SM-5) — it needs Jarad to add a device, then 14 days; and SM-2 is a feature-exists check ("100% ... appear") rather than an outcome. No metric covers the backward-compat promise (FR-13/NFR-2), fencing (FR-10), or the Event Log (FR-17/18). *Fix:* add an operational outcome such as median and p95 deploy wait before/after, and demote SM-5 to "checked when a device is added".

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
FR-1 through FR-17, FR-21 to FR-23 have verifiable consequences with numbers or specific refusals (60 seconds, 48 hours, 90 s lapse, exit codes 75/76/2, "exactly the expected event sequence"). FR-7's three consequences are a model of testable fairness. The gaps cluster in the later FRs and in a handful of adjectives and unspecified thresholds.

### Findings
- **high** Holocene FRs have no testable consequences (§4.6 FR-25, FR-26, FR-27, FR-28) — each is one sentence ("Jarad can register a discovered device ... edit it and retire it") with no condition that tells an engineer when it is done; FR-28 hangs on an unspecified "threshold" ("offline or absent longer than a threshold"). Only FR-24 has consequences. Since these are most of the user-visible surface, story creation will have to invent the criteria. *Fix:* add consequences (for example, registering from the page produces the same Registry state as FR-1 and the board shows the new Resource within 2 s; FR-27 window options and which numbers appear), and give the thresholds values or mark them assumptions.
- **medium** Thresholds and ranges named but not valued (FR-20 "waited longer than a threshold"; FR-8 "shorter than its job timeout"; FR-15 "a range"; FR-18 "utilisation (held, idle and away fractions)"; FR-12 "Reservations keep their place" while away) — FR-8 does not say what the new wait is (the 20-minute job timeout is stated, the wait is not); FR-15 does not say what the range means (percentiles? bootstrap interval?) though SM-4 depends on it; "adjusted by how early such Leases are usually released" is an undefined adjustment; FR-12 has no bound on how long a Reservation survives a Mac that stays away. *Fix:* give each a value or a definition, even as `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **low** FR-5 consequences are implementation notes, not behaviour (FR-5) — "Phones use the same `findPhone` matching that deploys use" is a design constraint; there is no staleness bound on "when it was last checked" and no refresh trigger. *Fix:* state maximum age of a Reachability reading shown as current.
- **low** FR-23 only covers short restarts (FR-23) — "a Hub API restart shorter than its Heartbeat lapse" leaves restarts longer than 90 s undefined. *Fix:* say what happens to remote Claimants' places then.

## Scope honesty — strong
§6 Non-Goals does real work (device farm, test orchestrator, priority classes, policy.json authority, purchase automation). §7.2 gives revisit triggers for deferrals, and the single `[NOTE FOR PM]` on "any free compatible device" is placed at a real deferral. Assumptions are inline, tagged and indexed; the 6-assumption/5-open-question count is proportionate for an internal Fast-path draft. §2.2 Non-Users clearly bounds hosts.

### Findings
- **medium** Two requirements with wide blast radius are folded into other FRs, so the scope reads smaller than it is (FR-10; §3 Claimant) — (a) the fencing token ("A deploy job's install and readback steps present their token, and a stale token is refused") changes the deploy install/readback code paths, yet it sits as the last consequence of "Leases with expiry, extension and Heartbeat", has no glossary entry, no UJ and no metric; (b) the catch-up, which the addendum (§A "The `adb` gap", `catchup.mjs:467`) names as a lock-taker that causes the very gap UJ-1 fixes, is not a Claimant kind (§3: "deploy run, an agent, or a person") and no FR says whether it queues. NFR-1 says the catch-up "calls" the same code, which does not say it waits in line. *Fix:* give fencing its own FR (or defer it explicitly) and state whether the catch-up queues or is a non-goal.

## Downstream usability — strong
Glossary is thorough and mostly used faithfully; IDs are contiguous and cross-references resolve (see Mechanical notes). Feature sections carry "Realizes UJ-n". UJ protagonists are named, including non-human ones (Tower's deploy run, an Earshot QA agent). The addendum properly keeps mechanism out of the PRD, though the PRD still leaks some (FR-5 `findPhone`, FR-4 "adb server", `HUB_LOCK_WAIT` format in FR-16, which is acceptable as an interface).

### Findings
- **low** Glossary missing terms the FRs rely on (FR-10, FR-13, §3) — "fencing token", "quarantine", "in jeopardy", "Job Hold" is defined but "hub-device" and "deploy-eligible" are only partly covered; "Control Surface" is defined but nearly every FR says "the page". *Fix:* add the first three; pick one of "Control Surface" or "the page".

## Shape fit — adequate
An internal tool with one operator would normally be a capability spec with light UJs. Here the four UJs are justified because each exercises a distinct Claimant (deploy run, agent, person), and UJ-1/UJ-2 carry edge cases that become tests. UJ-3 and UJ-4 are somewhat overhead but cheap. The over-formalization risk is not the UJs but the breadth: 28 FRs and 7 NFRs for one operator (see MVP slicing). Brownfield accuracy is good; line references are in the addendum and reference an older commit (`4cdefdc`; repo HEAD is later), which the addendum itself notes with "plus the hermetic-tests fix".

## Mechanical notes
- **IDs:** FR-1 to FR-28, NFR-1 to NFR-7, SM-1 to SM-5, SM-C1 to SM-C3 and UJ-1 to UJ-4 are contiguous with no duplicates. §7.1 FR ranges (1-6, 7-13, 14-16, 17-20, 21-23, 24-28) match §4. SM "Validates" references resolve. FR-20 and FR-28 are validated by no SM.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** all six inline assumptions appear in §10 (the Heartbeat/lapse and quarantine assumptions are merged into one index line; the NFR-6 inline tag has no text while the index entry adds "people reach it only through Holocene", which the inline NFR also states). The index is complete. The FR-9 assumption is duplicated by Open Question 1.
- **Glossary drift:** "Control Surface" / "the page" / "capacity panel" (UJ-4) / "Holocene resources page" (UJ-3); "waiter" (§4.3, SM-2) vs "queued Claimant"; "device" vs "Resource" in FR-4, FR-13 and §2.2; lowercase "lease" in UJ-1 ("a 30-minute lease") vs "Lease". Low impact.
- **Naming conflict to check:** FR-1 refuses names that collide with reserved words including `adb`, yet FR-3 seeds the S26 with lock name `adb`. The seed path presumably bypasses the check; say so.
- **FR-8 sentence is hard to parse:** "while it is first in line and the Resource is held only by Claimants behind it" — a holder is not behind the head of the queue; clarify which case is meant (likely a Hold granted out of order by a pinned older version).
- **Sections:** all expected sections for a single-operator capability spec are present (Vision, Target User, UJs, Glossary, Features/FRs, NFRs, Non-Goals, MVP scope, SMs, Open Questions, Assumptions).
